      How it works

      Paxos lets machines that can crash or lose messages agree on one value. Jean-Phillibert wants red, Jean-Maxence green and Jean-Eude blue. A value is chosen when three of the five acceptors (a majority) vote for it in the same round.

      A round has two steps. The proposer sends prepare, and each acceptor answers promise if it's the highest round she has seen, with her last vote if she has one. Otherwise she answers sorry. With three promises the proposer sends accept and waits for three votes. If one of the promises carried a vote, he has to propose that value instead of his own. That's why the proposers often end up deciding someone else's colour, and why they can never decide two different ones.

      A proposer gives up a round after three sorry, or after 2 seconds without an answer, then waits a bit and tries the next round.

      Things to try

      • Put the delay above 2000 ms, the proposer's timeout. In my original experiments, a delay of up to 5000 ms took 168 seconds and 14 rounds to reach a decision.
      • Crash two acceptors: the other three can still decide.
      • Crash three: nothing can be decided until one of them restarts and reads her state back from disk.
