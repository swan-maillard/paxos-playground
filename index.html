Paxy, a Paxos playground

Paxy was a lab from my Erasmus semester at UPC Barcelona in 2023: a Paxos simulation in Erlang/OTP where three proposers try to get five acceptors to agree on one colour. This page runs the same proposer and acceptor logic, message by message.

      What you're looking at

      Paxos is a way for machines that can crash or lose messages to agree on one value. Here, three proposers each want their own colour: Jean-Phillibert wants red, Jean-Maxence green and Jean-Eude blue. Five acceptors vote, and a value is chosen once three of them, a majority, have voted for it in the same round.

      A round has two steps. The proposer sends prepare, which asks "will you ignore every older round?". An acceptor answers promise if this is the highest round she has seen, and tells him the last value she voted for. If she already promised a higher round she answers sorry. With three promises, the proposer sends accept and waits for three votes. The catch is that if a promise carried an earlier vote, he has to propose that value, not his own. That's why the last proposer usually ends up deciding someone else's colour, and why two proposers can never decide different values.

      A round is a number plus the proposer's name, like (2, Jean-Eude), and two rounds with the same number are ordered by name, alphabetically. A proposer gives up a round after three sorry messages, or after 2 seconds without an answer. He then waits a random backoff (up to 10 ms, doubled after every failed round) and tries the next number.

      Things to try: in my original experiments, an injected delay of up to 5000 ms made the run take 168 seconds and 14 rounds. Push the delay past 2000 ms, the proposer's timeout, to see why. Crash two acceptors and the other three still decide. Crash three and nobody can, until one restarts and reads her promises back from disk.
